# M06, the connectome with its biophysics trained

**Consumes** the 721 luminances and nothing else, exactly as [M05](m05.md) does. Not the camera motion.

**Computes** a planar depth per column, the columns it refuses, and a boundary mask.

## The one thing that differs from M05

The wiring is the same measured MaleCNS optic lobe, with the same synapse counts and the same signs, and
they are never trained in either row. What changes is which of the remaining biophysical quantities
training may move:

| | M05, regime R0 | M06, regime R1 |
|---|---|---|
| topology, synapse counts, signs | frozen | frozen |
| resting potential, time constant | frozen (per cell type) | **trained**, per cell type |
| synaptic strength | frozen (per connected type pair) | **trained**, per connected type pair |
| trainable inside the network | 0 | **8,409** |
| the head | 12,883 parameters, trained | the same head, the same 12,883 parameters |

R1 is not an invention of this product: it is the regime of the published connectome-constrained model,
whose 734 trained parameters are of exactly these three kinds.

## Why it costs what it costs

In R0 a clip's activity depends only on the clip, so it was computed once and cached and every head then
trained on the cache. Here the gradient crosses the network and the whole time axis, so every optimiser
step simulates whole clips from scratch. Measured on this machine (RTX 4070 Laptop, 8 GB):

| Frames per clip | Batch | Seconds per step | Supervised frames per second | Peak memory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 4 | 0.354 | 90.5 | 2.78 GB |
| 16 | 4 | 0.578 | 110.7 | 4.43 GB |
| 16 | 8 | 1.951 | 65.6 | 8.65 GB |
| 32 | 2 | 0.592 | 108.0 | 4.36 GB |
| 32 | 4 | 1.943 | 65.9 | 8.41 GB |
| 32 | 8 | 18.372 | 13.9 | 16.65 GB |

The run uses **whole 32-frame clips at batch 2**. Batch 4 is the engine's own setting and is what the
table rejects: past about 4.5 GB this card spills and throughput halves, and at batch 8 it falls by a
factor of eight. Clips are always simulated whole, from the grey steady state, rather than as
sub-sequences, so no gradient ever starts from a mid-motion state the network never actually reached.

With the validation evaluation and the penalty's own backward pass, a step costs about 1.2 s in the real
run, so one seed of 300 steps is about 6 minutes. Each arm runs a three-point learning-rate grid and then
its remaining four seeds, and the four arms together took 3 hours 34 minutes of local GPU, plus 58 minutes
to cache the twenty trained networks over the calibration split and the cases. U6's twenty heads, trained
on a cache, took 13 minutes. That is the whole cost difference between the two units.

## Three things the published training does not hand over

**The head is warm-started from M05's.** A cold head needs about 1,500 optimiser steps, and here a step
simulates two clips rather than reading a cache. Measured with a cold head at the published rate, the
validation scale-invariant error was still bouncing between 6.4 and 7.8 at step 150 while M05's converged
heads sit at 3.5. Starting each seed from M05's own head for the same arm and seed, with literally the
same input statistics, turns the question into the one the unit asks: given the readout the frozen network
supports, what does letting the biophysics move buy? It also means the two rows can be read side by side,
because one begins where the other ended.

**The network's learning rate is chosen on validation, and it has to be relative.** Adam takes a step of
the size of the learning rate whatever the gradient is, and R1's three trainable groups differ by eighty
times: resting potentials have a median magnitude of 0.502, time constants 0.050, synaptic strengths
0.0063. At one absolute rate for all three, 600 steps move a synaptic strength by four times its own
median while a resting potential moves six percent, and the readout the head was fitted to is gone:
measured that way at the published 5e-5, validation went from 2.72 to 4.2 and never came back. Each group
is therefore trained at a rate scaled to its own size, and the relative rate is resolved per arm from the
grid {3e-5, 1e-4, 1e-3} on the VALIDATION split (1e-4 is the published 5e-5 as the resting potentials see
it), with the winning run kept as that arm's first seed and frozen for the arm's remaining seeds. Every
arm gets its own best rate, so no arm is handicapped by a number chosen for another, and no case clip is
read anywhere in that choice.

**The head's rate is a hundredth of U6's.** Warm-started from a converged head, U6's 3e-3 does not
fine-tune it, it destroys it. Measured with the network FROZEN, so that whatever moves is the head's own
rate alone, over 100 steps of validation error against M05's own 2.724: 3e-3 goes 2.86, 3.67, 4.09, 2.98;
3e-4 goes 2.84, 2.90, 3.00, 2.87; 3e-5 holds 2.728, 2.728, 2.733, 2.730. The cause is the batch and not
the head: U6 fitted on 16 independent frames per step, and a step here is 2 whole clips whose 64 frames
are correlated.

**The activity penalty keeps the published rate, and a measured baseline.** The engine penalises the
resting potentials when the central cells' mean activity strays from a baseline, and ties the penalty's
learning rate to the network's. Both parts had to be looked at:

- the published baseline is 5.0, which is where the published ensemble sits under its own stimuli. This
  product's transferred network on this product's renderings averages 0.67. Left at 5.0 the penalty stops
  being a guard and becomes the dominant force: in six steps it moved every resting potential by 11.3, on
  parameters whose whole range is 0.007 to 0.795. The baseline is therefore measured on each arm's
  starting network.
- tied to a network rate of 5e-3, the penalty's plain SGD overshoots its own quadratic and runs away:
  0.007, 0.108, 0.386, 2.514 over four steps. It keeps the published 5e-5 whatever the network trains at,
  because it is a slow guard and not a training signal.

## A trained arm's activity belongs to its seed

M05's five seeds share one cache per arm, because a frozen network's response to a clip is a property of
the arm. Here each seed trained its own network, so each has its own cache, written under
`R1-<arm>-seed<n>` by `run.py cache-trained` and read by `conectoma.methods.m06`. A seed whose cache is
missing is named in the report rather than averaged away silently.

Everything downstream is M05's, unchanged: the same refusal rule on the head's own predicted spread in log
depth, the same threshold chosen on the calibration split by the same criterion, the same median over five
seeds with their disagreement reported.

## How its claim is made

Never as its own number, and for M06 there are two comparisons, not one:

- **against its nulls** (N1 degree-preserving rewiring, N2 size-matched random sparse, N3 sign shuffle),
  each trained in the same regime with the same protocol and the same five seeds: does the measured wiring
  beat a control once both are allowed to fit their biophysics?
- **against M05 on the same clips**: does letting the biophysics move buy anything at all?

Both are paired per clip and aggregated with a bootstrap over clips, as every comparison in this product
is.

## What it measured

Twenty networks were trained, five seeds on each of the four arms, each starting from its own M05 head,
with the learning rate chosen per arm on the validation split. The chosen rates differ by arm, which is
the point of choosing them per arm: 1e-3 for the connectome, 1e-4 for N2 and N3, 3e-5 for N1.

On the VALIDATION split, the median over the five seeds of where each arm started and where it was kept
(scale-invariant error, lower is better):

| Arm | Rate | Started at | Kept at | What the regime bought |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the connectome | 1e-3 | 3.186 | 3.097 | +0.089 |
| N3 sign shuffle | 1e-4 | 3.495 | 3.289 | +0.206 |
| N2 random sparse | 1e-4 | 3.661 | 3.326 | +0.335 |
| N1 rewired | 3e-5 | 3.914 | 3.841 | +0.073 |

The ordering after training is the ordering before it, with narrower gaps, and one seed of the connectome
arm and one of N2 kept their starting point: no step of their run improved on the frozen network's own
readout, which is what the step-zero checkpoint is for.

On the CASES, the paired difference per clip at a fixed share of the lattice, positive meaning the first
row is better. M06's own coverage is the highest of any arm (median 0.72 against M05's 0.60), which is why
the comparison is not read on plain AbsRel:

| Comparison | Most confident 25% | Better half | Best 75% |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M06 against M05**, the same wiring frozen | +0.035 | **+0.059 [+0.052, +0.071]** | +0.089 |
| M06 against N1, a degree-preserving rewiring | +0.040 | **+0.052 [+0.042, +0.056]** | +0.106 |
| M06 against N3, the same wiring sign-shuffled | +0.041 | +0.010 [+0.000, +0.017] | -0.036 |
| M06 against N2, a size-matched random sparse graph | -0.004 | **-0.013 [-0.019, -0.010]** | -0.069 |

And what the regime bought each arm, which is the comparison that says whether the gain is about the
connectome at all:

| Arm | Most confident 25% | Better half | Best 75% |
|---|---|---|---|
| the connectome | +0.035 | **+0.059** | +0.089 |
| N2 random sparse | +0.045 | +0.038 | +0.037 |
| N3 sign shuffle | +0.017 | +0.026 | +0.030 |
| N1 rewired | +0.011 | +0.009 | +0.012 |

**The honest reading.** Three things, in the order a reader should take them.

1. **The regime buys the measured wiring the most.** Every arm improves when its biophysics may train, and
   the connectome improves by more than any null does over the better half and over three quarters of the
   lattice. The 8,409 parameters are worth more on the measured graph than on a scrambled one, which is a
   statement about the graph and not about the optimiser.
2. **The advantage over a degree-preserving rewiring grows with training**, from +0.010 in the reservoir
   to +0.052 here. U6's reading was that the degree structure carried the result; with the corpus
   corrected and the coverage matched, the specific wiring is ahead of its degree-preserving control in
   both regimes, and further ahead once the biophysics can move.
3. **A size-matched random sparse graph still beats it**, at every share and in both regimes, and no
   amount of training in this regime closes that. That is the negative result of this unit and it is not
   softened by the two positive ones: whatever depth-from-motion on this corpus rewards, a graph with no
   measured structure does it slightly better than the fly's own optic lobe once both are allowed to
   answer as much.

Against the floor, M06 sits at +0.745 [+0.637, +0.832] in plain AbsRel, slightly ahead of M05's +0.764
and still well behind the classical epipolar sweep (+0.265).

**Refusal.** M06 claims more than M05 everywhere, including where nothing is observable: on C13, pure
rotation, it refuses 16 percent of columns against M05's 30, and on C14, a static camera, 37 percent
against 44. A better readout of motion is also a more confident wrong answer when there is no motion to
read, and the unit reports that beside the improvement rather than under it.
