# M05, the connectome as a reservoir

**Consumes** the 721 luminances and nothing else. Not even the camera motion: the network is fed the
images and a small trained head reads its activity. That is worth stating twice, because it is also why
this row cannot know that a camera did not move.

**Computes** a planar depth per column, the columns it refuses, and a boundary mask.

## What is frozen and what is trained

Everything inside the network is frozen: the topology, the synapse counts, the signs, and the per-cell-type
biophysics. The only trained part is the head, and every arm of the comparison gets the same one.

```
the measured MaleCNS optic lobe  ->  T4a..T4d, T5a..T5d on 721 columns  ->  a small hex-space head
     (frozen, 2.94 M values)              (the cache)                     (12,883 parameters)
```

The head produces three channels: log depth, its own predicted spread in log depth, and a boundary logit.
The spread is what lets the row refuse a column, and it is trained rather than assumed: the depth loss is
the Gaussian negative log likelihood

```
L = mean( 0.5 exp(-2 s) e^2 + s ),    e = log z - log d,   s = log sigma
```

so a column the head cannot read is cheaper to mark wide than to guess. The threshold at which a wide
column becomes a refusal is chosen on the calibration split and frozen.

## Why the activity is cached

In this regime nothing inside the network is trained, so a clip's activity depends only on the clip. It is
computed once (0.12 s per clip on the laptop GPU in batches of four, 82 KB compressed) and every head then
trains on the cache without a gradient crossing the network or time. Five seeds times four arms is a job
of about an hour instead of days, and the readout sees exactly what the network produced rather than an
approximation of it.

### The key that was not unique

The first version of that cache was keyed by the clip's FILE NAME, and the numbers below are the ones the
corrected cache gives. The name `clip_000665.npz` occurs in many trajectories, and measured over the
committed split table:

| | Clips | Distinct file names | Clips that never reached the cache |
|---|---|---|---|
| train | 1,437 | 846 | **591 (41%)** |
| validation | 196 | 183 | 13 |
| calibration | 227 | 176 | 51 |

Worse, 199 names occur in more than one split, so a cache read for one split returned another split's
clip: 107 validation clips and 106 calibration clips resolved to a TRAIN clip's activity and its depth.
U4's leakage gate was green throughout and correctly so, because it proves the split TABLE has no family
overlap and a key collision happens downstream of it. The key is now the clip's place in the corpus
(source, environment, difficulty, trajectory, clip), `scripts/check_artifacts.py` re-derives every key
from the committed table and refuses a collision, and every head below was retrained on the corrected
corpus.

## The initialisation is not a detail

`R0` was specified as the connectome's wiring with the published model's per-cell-type biophysics
transferred where the types match (53 of 253 types). With the engine's DEFAULT initialisation instead, the
signal dies before it reaches the output units. Measured on a drifting pattern, standard deviation over
time:

| Cell type | Engine default | Published biophysics transferred |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | 0.005 | 0.086 |
| Tm3 | 0.0002 | 0.014 |
| T4a | 0.0001 | 0.039 |
| T5a | 0.00001 | 0.033 |

With the default initialisation an all-black clip and an all-white clip produce the same activity to four
decimals: a reservoir read from those cells would be measuring its own steady state. Every arm therefore
uses the transfer, and the cache stamp records it.

## How its claim is made

Never as its own number. The claim is the paired difference, per clip, against three controls built from
the same wiring and trained with the same head and the same five seeds:

- **N1**, degree-preserving rewiring: every cell type keeps its in- and out-degree.
- **N2**, a size-matched random sparse graph.
- **N3**, the same wiring with its signs shuffled.

A positive difference means the measured wiring did better than the control. An interval that crosses zero
means the measurement does not separate them, and that is reported as plainly as a positive result would
be: the plan has said since it was validated that a negative answer is a publishable outcome.

## What it measured

Twenty heads were trained, five seeds on each of the four arms, on the whole train split (1,437 clips,
every one of them distinct) with validation stopping. On the VALIDATION split the ordering is the
one the unit was built to test:

| Arm | Validation scale-invariant error, per seed | Median |
|---|---|---|
| the connectome | 2.65, 2.68, 2.72, 2.83, 2.84 | 2.72 |
| N3 sign shuffle | 2.84, 2.85, 2.92, 2.96, 2.96 | 2.92 |
| N2 random sparse | 2.89, 2.93, 2.94, 2.95, 2.98 | 2.94 |
| N1 rewired | 3.51, 3.54, 3.61, 3.64, 3.83 | 3.61 |

On the CASES, which is test data, the comparison is read in two ways and inside one domain at a time.
A network row is never told its own speed, so it cannot recover absolute scale: on the fly-scale cases it
answers in metres where the truth is centimetres, and a relative error pooled across domains measures
that failure rather than the wiring. So the headline is read on the TartanAir cases, the corpus the head
was trained on, and each row is read over the same share of its columns ranked by its own uncertainty,
because the arms refuse different amounts. Positive means the measured wiring did better.

**In domain (TartanAir, 288 paired clips), over the better half of the lattice:**

| Comparison | Relative error | Scale-invariant error |
|---|---|---|
| against N1, a degree-preserving rewiring | +0.016 [+0.012, +0.025] | **+0.000 [-0.004, +0.004]** |
| against N2, a size-matched random sparse graph | -0.025 [-0.034, -0.018] | **-0.003 [-0.008, -0.000]** |
| against N3, the same wiring with its signs shuffled | +0.035 [+0.022, +0.062] | **+0.009 [+0.005, +0.011]** |

**Out of domain, scale-invariant error only** (the relative error there measures scale):

| Comparison | Transfer | Fly scale | Synthetic controls |
|---|---|---|---|
| against N1 | +0.000 | +0.014 | +0.042 |
| against N2 | +0.002 | +0.044 | +0.032 |
| against N3 | +0.015 | +0.023 | +0.032 |

**The honest reading.** Frozen, the measured wiring recovers depth STRUCTURE on its own corpus no better
than a graph that keeps every cell type's degrees and scrambles the rest, and no better than a random
sparse graph of the same size; only the sign shuffle is reliably worse. Its small lead in relative error
over the rewiring is scale. Outside the corpus it does slightly better than all three controls, most
clearly on the synthetic controls and at fly scale, by a few hundredths of the scale-invariant error.
[M06](m06.md) is the same wiring with its biophysics allowed to train.

**How these numbers came to be the right ones.** Three defects stood between U6's first report and this
table, each found by looking at the engine rather than at the pages, each fixed with a test:

- the cache key described above, which cost the train split 41 percent of its clips;
- a paired AbsRel read across rows that refuse different amounts, where the ranking followed the
  calibrated thresholds rather than the accuracy;
- the scoring stage skipping this row on six cases (C04 and C08 to C12, 276 clips) for want of camera
  poses it never uses, so the transfer and ethological cases were never scored.

U6's published signs against N1 and N2 were both artefacts of the first two. A fourth, found on the
live site: pooling a relative error across domains the row cannot recover the scale of, which moved the
medians by hundreds on the fly-scale cases. The comparison is now read inside one domain at a time, with
the scale-invariant error beside it.

Against the floor, M05 sits at +0.764 [+0.747, +0.931] in plain AbsRel, which is better than the
correlator (+0.898) and the published network's own decoder (+0.894) and well behind the classical
epipolar sweep (+0.265).

**One failure mode a geometric row does not have.** On C13, pure rotation, M05 refuses only 30 percent of
columns and answers the rest, where every geometric row refuses all of them; on C14, a static camera, it
refuses 43 percent. It cannot know the camera did not move, because nothing in its input says so, and
that number belongs in the comparison as much as the depth error does.
