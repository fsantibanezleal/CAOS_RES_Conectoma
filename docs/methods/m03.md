# M03, the Hassenstein-Reichardt array

**Consumes** the 721 luminances and the camera motion, like M01. It is the fly's own motion detector,
untrained, read through the same inversion, so the difference between the two rows is the detector and
nothing else.

## The detector

Between neighbouring columns `a` and `b`, with `h` a first-order low-pass of time constant `tau`:

```
EMD_ab = L_a(t) h_b(t) - L_b(t) h_a(t)
```

Each arm multiplies one column's undelayed signal by its neighbour's delayed one and the mirror-symmetric
arms are subtracted, which is what makes the output signed and direction-selective. The input is the
luminance with its own mean removed, as the fly's input stage is high-pass. The lattice gives three
neighbour axes, at 27, 90 and 153 degrees in pixels (the engine's lattice is stretched along the row
axis), and a plane vector follows by least squares over the three.

## Three things this method has to admit

1. **A correlator is not a velocity sensor.** Its response follows the temporal frequency a pattern
   presents, so the same speed over a finer pattern gives a different answer. Measured on drifting
   gratings: at one speed, two periods give responses differing by more than 1.5 times.
2. **A single column's response fits nothing.** Fitting one gain from the per-column response to the true
   displacement explains less than the mean does (r2 = -0.14 on a synthetic scene). Averaged over
   neighbours it becomes something a gain fits: r2 = 0.33, 0.50 and 0.60 after one, two and three rings.
   That pooling is what the fly's wide-field cells do with the outputs of its detectors, and its cost is
   stated with the result: the depth M03 produces is smoothed over the pooled neighbourhood.
3. **The calibration does not transfer.** The gain fitted at one contrast is more than three times wrong
   at another (measured, contrast 0.35 against 0.05). That is a result of this unit, not an error to be
   tuned away, and it predicts where M03 must fail: the contrast case (C07) and the texture case (C16).

## The time constant

Not taken from the literature. The reviews that state one for the fly are paywalled and the secondary
summaries disagree, so `tau` is swept over a decade around the frame interval and chosen on a calibration
set generated for the purpose: synthetic planes with their own depths, contrasts and seed, never the
synthetic cases, which are test data. The sweep and the chosen value are written into the report that
used them.

## What it measured

Calibrated on that set (chosen `tau` 0.05 s, three rings of pooling, r2 = 0.58) and run on a synthetic
scene it was not fitted to: 40 percent of columns claimed, median relative error 59 percent.

Through the scoring stage, first level, two clips per case, beside the floor:

| Case | Coverage | AbsRel |
|---|---|---|
| C15 textured planes, nearest plane 0.5 m | 0.84 | 3.20 |
| C16 textureless surfaces | 0.97 | 0.48 |
| C13 pure rotation | refused 1.00 | not applicable |

C15's first level puts the nearest plane at 0.5 m, where a column moves 43 pixels between frames, more
than three lattice steps: far beyond what a correlator between neighbours 13 pixels apart can follow. The
number is not a defect to be fixed, it is the measurement this row exists to provide, and the ladder is
read against the floor rather than against zero.
