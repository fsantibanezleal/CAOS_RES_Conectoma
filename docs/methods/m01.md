# M01, depth and moving objects from motion parallax

**Consumes** the 721 luminances the eye receives, and the camera motion the clip commits. Nothing else, so
it is a native row: it sees exactly what the fly's eye sees.

**Computes** a planar depth per column per step, a flag for every column it refuses, and the columns whose
displacement does not belong to a rigid scene.

## How

1. **A sweep along the epipolar line.** With the camera motion known, a column's point can only land along
   one line, parameterised by inverse depth, so the unknown is one number rather than two. The sweep tries
   inverse depths uniformly, matching the column's seven-column patch, and keeps the best. The grid is
   sized from each step's own geometry so that no level moves the landing point by more than half a pixel.
2. **A two-dimensional refinement** from that answer, which lets the displacement leave the epipolar line.
   For a rigid scene it cannot, so the deviation from the line finds independently moving objects without
   knowing any depth.
3. **Refusal**, for either of two independent reasons: the match did not fix the depth (a flat or
   repeating patch leaves the sweep's cost curve level), or the geometry could not have fixed it (no
   baseline across the line of sight, which is every column under pure rotation and the column looking
   along the direction of travel).

Why a sweep rather than a differential estimator: measured on the corpus, the columns move 39 pixels
between consecutive frames at the median and 96 at the ninth decile, which is three and seven lattice
steps. A differential estimator's capture range is about one step, and on these clips its median endpoint
error equals the median displacement. The frame rate is the source's own, so this is a property of the
data, not a choice.

## What it measured

On a TartanAir forest clip, 32 frames, against the committed depth:

| Row | Columns claimed | Median relative error |
|---|---|---|
| the committed flow through the same readout (the floor) | 88 percent | 0.9 percent |
| M01 | 43 percent | 8.1 percent |

The floor row says the arithmetic after the flow costs almost nothing, so a motion method's error is its
flow's error and M01's distance from the floor is the part it owns.

Over the whole matrix, every case at every level, eight clips each (756 clips, 26 minutes on eight cores),
the paired difference in AbsRel against the floor is **0.265, with a 95 percent interval of 0.239 to
0.280, over the 376 clips both rows cover**. Per case, the median over the six levels:

| Case | Coverage | AbsRel |
|---|---|---|
| C01 forest flight | 0.25 | 0.370 |
| C02 urban street | 0.31 | 0.281 |
| C03 hospital corridor | 0.53 | 0.229 |
| C05 city at night | 0.50 | 0.260 |
| C06 motion blur | 0.42 | 0.295 |
| C07 marsh in fog | 0.08 | 0.690 |
| C15 textured planes | 0.52 | 0.160 |
| C16 textureless surfaces | 0.08 | 0.235 |

The fog case is the one to read twice: coverage falls to 8 percent, which is the method refusing where the
contrast is gone, and the error on what it still claims is the worst of the set. That is the shape of an
honest failure.

## Where it fails, and why that is the point

- **Below about a fifth of a lattice step** the displacement is read too small and the depth too large. A
  plane at 8 m with a 0.1 m baseline moves 2.7 pixels and comes back at 10.9 m. The reported uncertainty
  uses a flow noise of 0.8 pixels and predicts that error: 29 percent expected against 37 percent
  observed.
- **Pure rotation and a static camera** (cases C13 and C14) leave no baseline, and every column is
  refused. That refusal is the grade of those cases.
- **A patch that straddles a depth edge** matches poorly at every depth, and the sweep's uncertainty says
  so rather than returning one of the two depths as if it were sure.
